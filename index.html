Four steps, one review screen

No new editor to learn. The extension works on the template you already have and shows its work before it touches anything.

1

Open a job ad

On LinkedIn Jobs, right-click and choose Adapt to CV. The ad text is read from the page you are on.

2

Review the requirements

Each requirement is marked supported, partly supported or missing, with the CV text it is based on.

3

Approve the wording

Proposals appear as a word-level diff. Edit, reject or regenerate a section until it reads like you.

4

Get a new document

A copy of your template with only the approved text, checked against the diff before you open it.

Built to keep you in control

An adapted CV is only useful if you can defend every sentence in it. The extension is designed around that.

Evidence, not guesses

Every proposal points to the CV text behind it. Skills without evidence stay out unless you explicitly allow them.

You see the diff

Added and removed words are highlighted per section, so a rewrite never slips past you.

Your template stays yours

Sections are found by tags such as [ERFARENHET] or [KOMPETENS]. The original is never written to.

The copy is verified

The finished document is compared with the approved text. If anything differs, the copy is discarded instead of handed over.

Free-tier aware

Only Gemini models with a free standard tier are accepted. The daily counter and fallback order keep you inside the quota.

English and Swedish

The interface is in English by default and switches to Swedish in Settings. The language of the CV itself is a separate choice.

Privacy

No server in the middle

The extension talks directly to Google with your credentials. There is nothing to sign up for and no one else to trust with your CV.

  • ✓Your own API keyStored only in this Chrome profile, never synced and never sent anywhere except Google's API.
  • ✓Settings sync without secretsPreferences follow you between profiles; the API key and CV content do not.
  • ✓No analytics or remote codeNo telemetry, no advertising, nothing loaded from a third-party server.
  • ✓Delete everything in one stepSettings clears the cached analyses, the counter, the history and all local data.

What leaves your computer

The job ad text and the CV text are sent to the Google Gemini API for analysis and drafting, using your key.

Your template and the new copy are read and written through the Google Docs and Drive APIs with your OAuth token.

That is the whole list. No other server is contacted.

Set up in about a minute

You need Chrome 105 or later, a Google account and a free Gemini API key from Google AI Studio.

Before you start

  • Chrome 105+Any recent desktop version works.
  • A Google accountUsed only to create the copy in your own Drive.
  • A Gemini API keyCreated in Google AI Studio. The free tier is enough.

Install and connect

  1. Load the extensionOpen chrome://extensions, enable Developer mode and choose Load unpacked with the project folder.
  2. Paste your API keyOpen Settings and enter the Gemini key. It stays in this profile.
  3. Add your templatePaste the Google Docs link and run the template check so the tags are recognised.
  4. Adapt your first adOpen a LinkedIn job ad, right-click and choose Adapt to CV.

Questions

Does it overwrite my CV?

No. The extension creates a new document in your Drive and never writes to the template you added.

What happens to a skill the ad asks for but my CV does not mention?

It is marked as having no evidence and is left out. You can allow unverified skills in Settings, but they are then clearly marked and you are responsible for reviewing them.

Which Gemini models can I use?

Only models with a free standard tier. The extension rejects other model IDs and never switches to a paid model on its own.

What does the extension read?

Only the job ad you capture and the template you have added. It does not browse your Drive or read other tabs.

What does it cost?

The extension is free and open source. You use your own Gemini key, and Google's free tier covers normal use. Google may change its quotas over time.
